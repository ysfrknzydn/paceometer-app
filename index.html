About Paceometer

Instead of just showing your speed, Paceometer shows how much time speeding up would actually save you, plus a color so you can tell at a glance whether it's worth it — and where the posted speed limit is known, the app never suggests going faster than what's legal, no matter what the time math alone would say.

The pace number (minutes to cover 10 miles at your current speed) is based on a real published study (Peer & Gamliel, 2013), which found that showing drivers this number instead of just mph made them much better at judging how much time speeding up would actually save.

The color indicator: it reflects how much time going 10mph faster would actually save — one color means that gain is still meaningful, another means it's shrinking, another means there's basically nothing left to gain. A fourth, distinct color takes priority over all of that whenever you're at or above a posted speed limit the app knows about. How big a time savings counts as "meaningful" is adjustable in Settings under Zone Sensitivity — none of these specific cutoffs are from a published study; they're this app's own design choice (informed by the research team's guidance), chosen to land on clean, round numbers within the same math as the pace formula above.

The end-of-trip number is how much time you actually gained by driving faster than the posted speed limit, counted only over the parts of the trip where a limit was known. A small number is the good sign here — it means speeding barely bought you anything.

When it's available, this app looks up the posted speed limit near you (from OpenStreetMap) so it never tells you to go faster than what's legal. That data isn't always complete, and how safe a specific road actually is is still something this app doesn't know — a known limit changes the color, but it's never a promise that a road is safe to drive at that speed.

Privacy

Your raw GPS location is used two ways: entirely on your phone to calculate your speed in the moment, and briefly sent to OpenStreetMap's free public speed-limit lookup (a service called Overpass) so the app can tell what the posted limit is near you. That lookup is transient — it's never stored by Paceometer, never sent to our database, and never logged anywhere.

When you end a trip, only summary numbers are saved: average/min/max speed, distance, pace, and how much of the trip was spent where speeding up still meaningfully helped or at/under the posted speed limit. No route and no coordinates ever leave your phone or reach our database.

Only you can see your own trips — the database is locked down so each signed-in driver can only read their own data.

The voice feedback button records a short clip only while you're actively using it, sends it once to be transcribed into text, and then the audio itself is discarded — it's never saved anywhere, by us or in transit. Only the resulting text is kept.

Appearance

Follows your phone's light/dark setting by default. Override it here if you'd like.

Units

Imperial (mph, miles, $/gallon) or metric (km/h, km, $/liter) for the speed, pace, distance, and gas-price displays. The always-on speed limit sign stays mph either way — it's meant to match the real posted sign, which is always in mph on US roads.

Zone Sensitivity

How much time speeding up must save before the app calls it worthwhile. Standard is the project's default; Strict and Strictest ask for a bigger payoff before agreeing it helps, which pushes the "no time left to gain" state to kick in at a lower speed.

Sound

Chimes for zone changes and starting/ending a trip. Mute if they'll clash with music or a call — the haptic buzz (on supported phones) still works either way.

Vehicle & Gas Cost

Pick your vehicle so the end-of-trip summary can estimate roughly what the trip cost in gas, and roughly how much of that came from driving over the speed limit. This uses EPA city/highway MPG figures for your exact year/trim, not real-time engine data, and only approximates aerodynamic drag above highway speed — treat it as a ballpark, not an exact number.
